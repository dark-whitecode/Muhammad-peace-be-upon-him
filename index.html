Who he was

Muhammad was an Arab religious, military, and political leader, and the founder of Islam. According to Islam, he was the final prophet of God, divinely inspired to preach and confirm the monotheistic teachings of Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus and other prophets in Islam.

He is believed by Muslims to be the Seal of the Prophets. Along with the Quran, his teachings and normative examples form the basis for Islamic religious belief.

Early life

According to the traditional account, Muhammad was born in Mecca to the aristocratic Banu Hashim clan of the Quraysh. He was the son of Abdullah ibn Abd al-Muttalib and Amina bint Wahb. His father died around the time Muhammad was born.

His mother Amina died when he was six, leaving Muhammad an orphan. He was raised under the care of his grandfather, Abd al-Muttalib, and later his paternal uncle, Abu Talib.

The first revelation

In later years, he would periodically seclude himself in a mountain cave named Hira for several nights of prayer. When he was 40, in c. 610, Muhammad reported being visited by Gabriel in the cave and receiving his first revelation from God.

In 613, Muhammad started preaching publicly, proclaiming that “God is One”, and that complete “submission” (Islām) to God (Allāh) is the right way of life (dīn).

He said he was a prophet and messenger of God, similar to other prophets in Islam.

Persecution and migration

Muhammad’s followers were initially few in number, and experienced persecution by Meccan polytheists for 13 years. To escape it, he sent some of his followers to the Aksumite Empire in 615, before he and his followers migrated from Mecca to Medina (then known as Yathrib) in 622.

This event, the Hijrah, marks the beginning of the Islamic calendar, also known as the Hijri calendar. In Medina, Muhammad united the tribes under the Constitution of Medina.

Return to Mecca

In December 629, after eight years of intermittent fighting with Meccan tribes, Muhammad gathered an army of 10,000 Muslim converts and marched on the city of Mecca. The conquest went largely uncontested, and he seized the city with minimal casualties.

In 632, a few months after returning from the Farewell Pilgrimage, he fell ill and died. By the time of his death, most of the Arabian Peninsula had converted to Islam.

His legacy

The revelations (waḥy) that Muhammad reported receiving until his death form the verses (āyah) of the Quran, upon which Islam is based. Muslims regard them as the verbatim word of God and his final revelation.

Beyond the Quran, his teachings and practices are also upheld and used as sources of Islamic law. Apart from Islam, Muhammad plays a significant role in the Druze faith as one of the seven main prophets, and in the Baháʼí Faith as a Manifestation of God.

Key terms

Quran

The book of revelations Muhammad reported receiving until his death.

Hadith

Transmitted reports of Muhammad’s teachings and practices.

Sīrah

The biography of Muhammad.

Hijrah

The 622 migration from Mecca to Medina that begins the Islamic calendar.
